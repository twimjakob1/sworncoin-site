Rules

17 rules, in plain English.

These are the rules the tested model enforces. Every number in them is a proposal until launch.

The rules

Rule 10 describes a bond in dollars. That bond is in the model and is left out of the first launch, where every bond is in coins.

The coin

  1. 1The supply is 1,000,000,000 coins, created once.
  2. 2No one can create coins, freeze an account, reverse a transfer or change a balance. This includes the founder.

The founder

  1. 3Checks that involve the founder or the founder’s company add nothing to the founder’s usage milestone.
  2. 4Coin owners with 20% of the coins outside the founder’s circle can block a founder release during its notice period.

Checks, fees and challenges

  1. 5A checker posts at least 10,000 coins before verifying anything.
  2. 6The party who posts a claim pays a fee in coins, 20 at the least. 80% pays the checker whose finding stands, and 20% goes to the community pool.
  3. 7When a challenge overturns a finding, the checker’s share of the fee pays the challenger.
  4. 8A claim that no checker takes up within 30 days can be withdrawn. The fee and the bond return in full.
  5. 9A check reads the real system. The operator’s own statement carries no weight.
  6. 11A false finding costs the checker 50% of its stake, split between the challenger and the community pool.
  7. 12A finding can be challenged for 7 days. A failed challenge costs the challenger 100 coins.
  8. 13A finding is judged against the system as it stood on the day the finding was made.

Claims of finished work

  1. 10Work reported finished that the system shows unfinished sends the operator’s dollar bond to the customer.

Sworn statements

  1. 14An issuer can post a sworn statement about its own coin for 30 to 365 days, with a bond of at least 10,000 coins behind it. A statement that reads true at its first check is in force for the term.
  2. 15Anyone can report a statement in force. A statement that reads false forfeits its bond: 20% to the reporter and 80% to the community pool. A report against a statement that still reads true costs the reporter 100 coins, paid to the issuer.
  3. 16A statement that stands through its term is recorded as honored, and its bond returns to the issuer.

The ledger

  1. 17Every finished check is entered in a ledger where each receipt carries the fingerprint of the one before it.

The founder schedule

The founder’s 100,000,000 coins sit in a vault. Nothing leaves for 12 months. The coins then leave in 36 monthly slices of 2,777,777.

Months 1 to 12: locked
Months 13 to 48: one slice a month
  • The calendar alone releases nothing. Each slice also requires 500 more independent verified checks.
  • Checks that involve the founder or the founder’s company add nothing to that count.
  • Each release is announced 30 days ahead. During those days, owners of 20% of the coins outside the founder’s circle can block it.

Risks

  • Sworn carries no fixed value and no promise of redemption.
  • The registry code will have no owner key, so a fault found after launch stays.
  • A dispute is settled by reading the system again. Sworn therefore covers only systems that anyone can read, and public blockchain data is the first.
  • The minimum fee and the minimum bond are set in coins. A large rise in the coin’s price makes them costly, and a large fall makes them trivial.
  • Federal rules for coins leave several questions open. Congress could pass a statute that changes the answers.
