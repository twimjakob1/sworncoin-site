How Sworn compares.

Putting a bond behind a claim is an established idea, and several projects do it today. This page names them and says where Sworn differs.

Where Sworn differs

  • Settlement

    A dispute is settled by reading the blockchain again, and the finding is judged against the system as it stood on the day the finding was made. Owning a large share of a coin gives no power over the result.

  • Duration

    A sworn statement is watched for its whole term, 30 to 365 days. An audit or a scan describes the code on the day it was read.

  • Scope

    A statement can be any fact about the issuer’s own coin that a checker can read: the supply, the power to freeze, what insiders own, the trading pool, who can change the rules.

  • Cost of a lie

    The issuer loses the bond, at least 10,000 Sworn, and the record shows the statement as broken. A checker who records a false finding loses 50% of its stake.

Where the others are ahead

  • They are in use today, with real users and real money behind them. Sworn is in testing.
  • Their code has been reviewed and attacked by outsiders for years. No one outside the project has reviewed Sworn’s code yet.
  • UMA and Kleros can settle questions about the world outside a blockchain. Sworn covers only what anyone can read from one.

They answer different questions

A lock shows that liquidity cannot leave before a date. An audit shows what experts found in the code on one day. A scan shows what software reads today. A sworn statement shows what the issuer stands to lose if a stated fact changes.

A careful buyer can use all four.

Sworn’s code is public for anyone who wants to check these claims.
