Questions

Questions and answers.

Short answers, each drawn from the rules and the record.

What is Sworn?

Sworn is a coin for proving that a claim is true. Someone posts a claim and locks coins behind it. An independent checker reads the system the claim is about and records what it shows. Anyone can challenge that finding for 7 days.

Why would the issuer of a coin use Sworn?

A promise that costs nothing to break tells a buyer little. An issuer that locks at least 10,000 Sworn behind a statement loses those coins if the statement turns false during its term. An issuer that keeps its word gets the bond back, and the record shows the statement as honored.

How is this different from a scanner or an audit?

A scanner reports what its software reads, and it can misread: in this project’s test of ten well-known coins, a public scanner misread one and could not read another. An audit describes the code on the day of the audit. A sworn statement covers a term of 30 to 365 days, and the issuer loses the bond if the fact changes during it.

Can I buy Sworn?

No coin called Sworn is for sale today. Sworn is in testing and exists on a practice chain only. Any coin offered for sale under the Sworn name today is a counterfeit. The project sells no coins, now or at launch.

Who is behind Sworn?

Sworn is a registered name of VitalEdge, LLC, a Tennessee company. The founder is Jakob Taylor.

What does the founder receive?

100,000,000 coins, which is 10% of the supply. They are locked in a vault for 12 months and then leave in 36 monthly slices. Each slice also requires 500 more independent verified checks and a 30-day public notice, and owners of 20% of the coins outside the founder’s circle can block a release.

Can anyone create more coins or freeze mine?

The supply is 1,000,000,000 coins, created once. The rules give no one, the founder included, the power to create coins, freeze an account, reverse a transfer or change a balance.

What can Sworn check today?

Facts that anyone can read from a public blockchain, such as whether a coin’s code can create more coins. Work inside a private system, such as an inbox, has no solution in this build.

What stops a checker from lying?

A checker posts at least 10,000 Sworn before checking anything. Anyone can challenge a finding for 7 days, and a false finding costs the checker 50% of that stake. A party to a statement cannot check it.

What stops false reports and challenges?

A reporter posts 100 Sworn with a report. When the statement still reads true, those coins go to the issuer. A challenger posts 100 Sworn, and a failed challenge costs the challenger those coins.

How will coins reach the public?

Half of the supply, 500,000,000 coins, is set aside to be given at no charge. The rule for that distribution has not been written. Checkers also receive coins as pay for checks.

When does Sworn launch?

No date is set. The rules run today in a tested model and as blockchain code on a practice chain.

How do I take part in testing?

Issuers build a statement on the Take Part page and send it. Checkers write to hello@sworncoin.com with the chains and contract languages they read. Readers can ask the issuer of a coin to put a bond behind a promise. Taking part pays no coins and gives no claim to any later distribution.

Which channels are official?

This site, sworncoin.com; the X account @sworncoin; and the address hello@sworncoin.com. Any message that asks for payment or for a wallet’s recovery words in Sworn’s name is a fraud.
