How It Works

A statement, a bond, a check and a challenge.

The first claims Sworn checks come from the issuers of other coins. An issuer states a fact about its own coin and puts Sworn behind it. The fact sits on a public blockchain, where anyone can read it again.

The four steps

  1. Post

    An issuer posts a sworn statement about its own coin for a term of 30 to 365 days. A bond of at least 10,000 Sworn goes behind the statement, with a fee of at least 20 Sworn for its check.

  2. Check

    A checker who has posted at least 10,000 Sworn of its own reads the coin’s code and records a finding. A party to the statement cannot check it.

  3. Challenge

    Anyone can challenge the finding for 7 days. A false finding costs the checker 50% of its stake. A failed challenge costs the challenger 100 Sworn.

  4. Settle

    A statement that stands through its term is recorded as honored and its bond returns. A statement shown false forfeits its bond: 20% to the reporter and 80% to the community pool.

One statement, start to finish

An issuer swears for 365 days that no more of its coins can be created, then breaks the statement.

  1. Day 0

    The issuer posts the statement and locks 10,000 Sworn behind it. A checker reads the code and records that the statement is true.

  2. Day 7

    No one has challenged the finding. The statement is in force for 365 days.

  3. Day 200

    The issuer changes its code so that new coins can be created. A reporter posts 100 Sworn and reports the statement. It now reads false.

  4. Same day

    The reporter receives 2,000 Sworn. 8,000 Sworn go to the community pool. The record shows the statement as broken.

Had the issuer left the code alone, the 10,000 Sworn would have returned when the term ended and the record would show the statement as honored.

Statements an issuer can swear to

Each is a fact about the issuer’s own coin that a checker can read from the blockchain.

  • “No more of our coins can be created.”The checker reads the coin’s code for any function that creates coins.
  • “No account can freeze or take your coins.”The checker reads the code for any power to freeze, pause or change a balance.
  • “Insiders own no more than 10% of the supply.”The checker reads the balances of the creator and the controlling accounts.
  • “The trading pool cannot be withdrawn this year.”The checker reads who controls the pool and whether it is locked.
  • “No one can replace the code.”The checker reads whether any account has the power to change the rules.

Who does what

The coin has four jobs: an issuer’s bond, a checker’s stake, the fee for a check, and the bond on a report or a challenge.

  • A reader

    You are deciding whether to trust a coin. You see which of its promises have coins behind them, which were honored and which were broken. Reading requires no Sworn.

  • An issuer

    You run a coin and want your promises about it believed. You post a statement for 30 to 365 days, lock a bond of at least 10,000 Sworn behind it and pay a fee of at least 20 Sworn. When the statement stands through its term, the bond returns and the record shows the statement as honored.

  • A checker

    You verify statements. You post at least 10,000 Sworn as your stake, read the coin and record a finding. You receive 80% of the fee for each finding that stands. A false finding costs 50% of your stake.

  • A reporter

    You find a statement that has turned false. You post 100 Sworn with your report. When the statement reads false, you receive 20% of its bond. When it still reads true, your 100 Sworn go to the issuer.

Why the bond is a coin

Code can lock a coin without any company taking custody of it. Bonds, stakes and fees go from a wallet into the registry’s code and come out under its rules.

A dispute is settled by reading the system again. The finding is judged against the system as it stood on the day the finding was made.

Claims of finished work

The tested model also covers a second kind of claim: a contractor, a service or an AI agent reports that a job is finished, for example that a report was sent or an invoice was filed. A checker reads the system where the work would appear.

This kind of claim is left out of the first launch. It works today only where the checker can read the system, and work inside a private system, such as an inbox, has no solution in this build.
