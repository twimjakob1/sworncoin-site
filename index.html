What Sworn is for

A promise costs nothing to make. The issuer of a coin can say that its supply is fixed, and a buyer has only the issuer’s word for it.

Sworn puts coins behind the promise. The issuer locks a bond. An independent checker reads the coin and records what it shows. If the statement turns false during its term, the issuer loses the bond and the record shows the statement as broken.

A buyer reads the result at no charge: which promises have coins behind them, and which were honored.

What a sworn statement adds

The tools people use today make mistakes. This project graded ten well-known coins from a public scanner’s data. The scanner misread one coin and could not read another.

Four steps

One sworn statement, from posting to settlement.

  1. Post

    An issuer states a fact about its own coin and locks a bond behind it.

  2. Check

    An independent checker reads the coin and records what it shows.

  3. Challenge

    Anyone can dispute the finding for 7 days.

  4. Settle

    A statement that stands returns its bond. A statement shown false loses it.

Who uses Sworn

The coin has four jobs: an issuer’s bond, a checker’s stake, the fee for a check, and the bond on a report or a challenge.

  • A reader

    You are deciding whether to trust a coin. You see which of its promises have coins behind them, which were honored and which were broken. Reading requires no Sworn.

  • An issuer

    You run a coin and want your promises about it believed. You post a statement for 30 to 365 days, lock a bond of at least 10,000 Sworn behind it and pay a fee of at least 20 Sworn. When the statement stands through its term, the bond returns and the record shows the statement as honored.

  • A checker

    You verify statements. You post at least 10,000 Sworn as your stake, read the coin and record a finding. You receive 80% of the fee for each finding that stands. A false finding costs 50% of your stake.

  • A reporter

    You find a statement that has turned false. You post 100 Sworn with your report. When the statement reads false, you receive 20% of its bond. When it still reads true, your 100 Sworn go to the issuer.
